## TLDR

- **Decision models: the first independent evidence is in, and it is mixed.** A University of Minnesota paper, SanSi (arXiv:2610.07730), builds a 1.4B looped decision model and, as a side result, tests the Jev API (`jev-1.13.0`) on its 10,027-decision suite: **78.9%** accuracy, above every model the authors trained (their best, SanSi-2.6B, 75.8%), and **83.5%** on sources none of their models saw. But the same paper reports Jev falling on multi-step "liar chain" puzzles from **100% at one step to 62.5% at three and chance (50%) from six steps on**, and says 19.7% of SanSi's own errors carry confidence of 0.9 or more, a warning for anyone gating actions on probabilities. Separately, a 1.7B Qwen model constrained to option tokens reaches 59% on CommonsenseQA and, after temperature scaling, a calibrated confidence curve (Nish Tahir, HN **382**). ## Trends / topics / directions

**The meta moved in three places since Oct 10:** (1) *decision models* went from "funded and crowded" to **being audited and copied**: independent papers, DIY tutorials and open reimplementations (Kev, SanSi) appeared within weeks of Jev's Sept 15 launch, and the incumbent teased a next release; (2) *agent accountability* shifted from "will the government force disclosure?" to **"who is the independent checker, and who pays?"**; (3) a new capital theme appeared: **training grounds for models (RL environments) and the evaluator layer** as a business. The Oct 9–10 themes (agent harness control plane, Lean/formal verification, cheap small models) continue; cheap-model discourse is no longer on the HN front page.

**Founder take (delta vs Oct 10):** *Build:* if you ship a decision model into a workflow, add two things this week: an eval slice with your own labels, including multi-step cases (Jev's drop to chance by six steps is the thing to test), and an input-injection test where user text claims the decision was already made; Pulumi's jev-router shows the pattern (guard questions that can only raise a tier, hard rules in code before the model is asked). *Ignore/hedge:* any adoption number for Jev (three company-side figures now: a third, 25%, 29.4%) and any private-company revenue claim from the RL-environment startups. *Dig into:* the evaluator and environment layer: if you build agents, buyers will ask for third-party test evidence; if you build infra, the demand signal for sandboxes, graders and reward-integrity tooling is visible in both Beam's post and the funding. *Watch:* TypeSafe's "next week" release and the Nvidia–Reflection outcome.

### Papers / discourse

- **SanSi** ([arXiv:2610.07730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07730); Gan, Lee, Kang, University of Minnesota). Turns a pretrained *looped* LM (Ouro 1.4B) into a typed decision model by reading option probabilities after every loop. 72.0% accuracy at eight loops on 10,027 decisions from 59 sources (13.5 points above a same-shape non-looped model; 1.8 below a 3x larger one at 7.7x the compute of one pass); three loops give 88% of the gain; calibration is best at loop 3 and gets worse after answers settle; averaging loop probabilities halves ECE. Jev API reference: 78.9% overall, 83.5% on unseen sources, **but 87.9% on JevBench items vs 79.4% for SanSi-2.6B**. The paper's reference list cites several other Jev audits (e.g. arXiv 2609.37647 "Evaluating and Benchmarking the System One Model Jev", 2609.35342 Sys1Cal calibration, 2609.26758 "Type-Safe Is Not Error-Free", 2609.32160 "Typed Decision Models: An Early Evidence Audit", 2609.33209 probability-axioms test); I did not open them. The authors note their suite is English, built from public datasets, and that gains depend on a looped pretrained backbone.
- **AgentMonBench / EBG** ([arXiv:2610.06406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06406); Renmin University / Kuaishou). A 300-instance benchmark for monitors that flag consequential agent decisions (omitted requirements, silent semantic changes that still pass tests, delayed user pushback). EBG, a training-free evidence graph, improves identification and localization for most of eight models, but absolute scores stay low ("considerable room for improvement"). Five Codex-harness cases show leakage, changed seeding and unequal search budgets being disclosed with the monitor on.
