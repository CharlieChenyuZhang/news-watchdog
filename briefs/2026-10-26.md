## What to read / what to watch

1. **Anthropic S-1** (Oct 26, institutional, top pick). Read MD&A (compute cost vs revenue, NRR, ≥$100K cohort), the dual-class / Class N / LTBT governance section, and the risk factors, which name existential risk and say models are "contributing to the pace of our own research." It's the first audited frontier-lab P&L. ([EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm))
2. **Max Woolf (minimaxir), "OpenAI and Anthropic cut their LLM prices by up to 80% within a few months. Why?"** (Oct 25, **New creator**). A dated timeline of cuts (GPT-5.6 Luna −80% Jul 30; GPT-6 Sol $2/$10; Opus 5.5 $4/$20). It uses Vercel gateway data showing cost per token at 43.3% of April's level while total spend rises, and cache reads at 96–99% of agent spend. He argues open weights and inference competition keep pushing prices down, and that moats are moving to enterprise commitments and personal agents. HN 380 / 382 comments. ([minimaxir.com](https://minimaxir.com/2026/10/cheap-llms/))
3. **Drew Breunig, "The State of Context Engineering"** (Oct 24, AGNTCon talk write-up, **New creator**). It covers four failure modes (poisoning, distraction, confusion, clash) and six fixes, then adds "fighting the weights" and "context inertia." It cites Anthropic's Memento harness, which clears context every 90 minutes, and MSR's Fork-and-Flush. He argues 2027's problem is memory and skill rot. HN 220. ([dbreunig.com](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/24/the-state-of-context-engineering.html))
4. **Anthropic, "Building agents: when to fold subagents into your main agent loop"** (recent; the page shows no date; institutional). Practical guidance: keep tools under ~30–50 definitions, group tools by user intent, have teams own skills rather than agents, and run each eval task more than once because breaks are flaky. ([Claude](https://claude.com/resources/articles/building-agents-when-to-fold-subagents-into-your-main-agent-loop))
5. **Dr Magnus Lynch, "A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis"** (Oct 25, **New creator**). On 200 biopsy-proven MILK10k lesions, Inception v3 scored 62% vs 42% for both frontier models. He includes caveats (small n, contamination) and a downloadable eval. It's a clean argument for narrow models in regulated verticals. HN 619. ([lynchlab.net](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer))
6. **Christian Keil (a16z), "Superhuman Hardware Design Is the Easy Part"** (Oct 20, institutional). The interface between digital and physical is lossy: models will soon do CAD and PCB design, but DFM, testing, supply chains and "process knowledge" remain the bottleneck. Demand moves to parts and test vendors. ([a16z.news](https://www.a16z.news/p/superhuman-hardware-design-is-the))
