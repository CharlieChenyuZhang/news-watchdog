## What to read / what to watch

1. **a16z, "The New Security Agenda"** (Oct 28, institutional survey; Stephenie Zhang and Owen Colby). They surveyed 100 CISOs at companies with 1,000+ employees: 87% expect security spend to rise in 2027, and **all seven of the most-selected 2027 priorities involve AI**. 55% already run MCP servers or agent integrations in production, and 46% worry about agents exceeding their permissions. Only 18% use AI SOC tools in production, but 45% plan to buy internal AI-agent security within 12 months. 36% let agents act autonomously on low-risk actions today, and 60% expect to within a year. The startup opening: 47% would pick a startup over their incumbent only to fill a gap the incumbent can't. Timely given this week's breaches. ([a16z](https://a16z.com/the-new-security-agenda/))
2. **Sashank Gondala (FurtherAI CTO), "Building an AI-native engineering org"** (Oct 29, X article, **New creator**, HN 169). Month by month, his team moved code, tests, AI review (99% of changes), CI (20x more tests in the same 17 min), bug intake (ClaudeTag files 60% of bug tickets), rules (21 commit-check rules that turn tribal knowledge into gates) and auto-merge (up to a third of changes merge with no human) over to agents. The result was ~5x merged changes per engineer with a revert rate under 0.5%. The catch: planning and review grew to 70% of the time, and he now defines seniority as how much of a problem someone owns end to end. ([X](https://x.com/sgondala2/status/2115832070274723875))
3. **Lucas Firmo, "I'm migrating a website with a fleet of AI agents"** (Oct 14, essay; first time in these briefs; **2nd high-signal hit, now promoted to standing radar**). He moves a Gatsby site to Next.js one route at a time, one agent per page, at most 5 at once and never two on the same file. A route is "done" only when the agent proves parity with screenshots at two widths, a no-JS render and byte-for-byte API-call captures (18/18 matched on onboarding). A different model reviews every PR; the giving pages took 11 rounds and one round caught dollars sent where the API expected cents. The postmortems are the useful part: a page that looked fine triggered third-party rate limits, ten agents sharing one folder all resumed the same session, and an agent trusted a stale doc. His rule: fix the rule, not the page. ([Bits with Lucas](https://www.bitswithlucas.com/blog/website-migration-with-ai-agents/))
4. **SigNoz (Elizabeth), "AI isn't replacing SREs, it's deskilling them"** (dated Feb 28, 2026, but **HN 612 this week** and first time in these briefs). It applies Bainbridge's 1983 *Ironies of Automation* to incident response: the more routine work AI absorbs, the less practice humans get for the novel failures automation can't handle. It cites endoscopists' unassisted polyp detection dropping from 28% to 22% after a period of AI use, and introduces "never-skilling" for juniors. Proposed fixes: deliberately run some incidents by hand, and keep humans working the problem rather than just approving. ([SigNoz](https://newsletter.signoz.io/p/ai-isnt-replacing-sres-its-deskilling))
5. **Chris Wellons, "Prospero: a new coding harness for a more civilized age"** (Oct 31, essay, **New creator** to these briefs; nullprogram/w64devkit). A C++ harness that uses roughly 100x fewer resources than web-stack harnesses. It brings no models of its own (llama.cpp, OpenRouter, the Anthropic or OpenAI APIs). Design rule: "Never wait forever" (every operation has a deadline, timed-out shell commands become async background jobs, and a command's whole process tree is killed together). It also has headless remote engines, desktop control, INI config, subagents, MCP and worktrees, in Community and Professional editions. A useful counterpoint to heavy harnesses. ([nullprogram](https://nullprogram.com/blog/2026/10/31/))
