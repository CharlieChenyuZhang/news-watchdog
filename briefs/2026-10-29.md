## What to read / what to watch

1. **Victor Martin Garcia, "How I Build with AI Coding Agents: What Changed in Eight Months"** (Oct 27, essay, **New creator**, found via Layer 2). He no longer reviews plans or approves merges; he agrees on a goal and a few constraints, and a "harness" (shared conventions, local runs, tests, logs, Grafana, Sentry, blue/green deploys) decides when a change is ready. A scheduled job watches Sentry, and an agent investigates, fixes and redeploys without him. He keeps humans only on critical paths, hard-to-undo decisions (with a second model as adversarial reviewer) and any change that weakens a check. In 30 days on one laptop: ~20 projects, ~600 commits, ~170 deploys; his conclusion is that his own attention is now the limit. ([codeisnolongerwritten.com](https://codeisnolongerwritten.com/writing/how-i-build-with-ai-2026-10))
2. **The Call Center Doctors, "16,885 changes in six months: how a fleet of AI agents builds our software"** (Oct 22, long-form case study, **New creator**). A non-software company runs an AI "front desk," up to 40 helper agents, a file-claim sheet and 28 guards. Every important change needs **two fresh AI reviewers to say SHIP on the exact version** (median $1.07 and 4.5 minutes per reviewer), after planted-bug tests show where coverage is thin (tests caught only 55%). 44% of merges land overnight; 16% of jobs were sent back at least once. Unusually honest about its gaps, including a tool update that made 47% of helper tokens a copied parent conversation. ([CCDocs](https://ccdocs.com/how-we-build-software-with-ai-agents/))
3. **Y Combinator, Requests for Startups, Winter 2027** (institutional, surfaced Oct 28). The theme is AI's bottlenecks moving past the models: space startups for cheap Starship launch, "the $15-an-hour expert," US-made robots and their supply chain, **LLMs that write well**, Plaid for government benefits, financing for the physical buildout, **vertical foundation models** (Diana Hu cites Jev's typed decisions), AI for City Hall, the data-center supply chain, air-gapped AI, and **"Own Your Intelligence."** Worth reading before the Nov 2 deadline. ([YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs))
4. **Amazon, "An update from Amazon engineering leadership on the role of the developer in the age of AI"** (Oct 28, institutional). Engineers are judged on customer, business and team outcomes, not AI usage, token counts or lines of code; adoption metrics may inform reviews but must not become targets. The line Willison quoted: "If your work does not benefit from current AI tooling, that is a finding, not a failing." Read it as the large-company counterweight to "tokenmaxxing." ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-software-engineer-role-guideline-ai) · [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/28/amazon-token-maxxing-ai-work/))
5. **GitHub Octoverse 2026 + Universe keynote** (Oct 28, institutional report). The numbers behind "agentic engineering": merged PRs tripled to 220M a month, commits 7.38B a month, over 40% of PRs began from an agent draft by September, and Copilot cloud-agent usage rose 230%. ([Octoverse](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/) · [Willison's notes](https://simonwillison.net/))
